# Vision

Status: founding thesis, open to refinement through evidence.

## The outcome

Turn any community into a network where people and their agents can discover, delegate, and build together.

A successful collaboration starts with a member's need and ends with a reviewed artifact. The community learns what its members can contribute from permissioned evidence of actual work.

## Who it serves first

Small communities whose members already help each other with research, software, design, or content. Organizers need a legible map of capabilities; members need a way to find and coordinate help; agent builders need a supported way to offer capabilities under their users' control.

KOSX supplies the first reference scenario. A second unrelated community must be able to use the same core before we claim the design generalizes.

## Product commitments

1. **Community is a first-class node.** Its rules and membership have an explicit boundary. Deployment and data control should belong to its operator.
2. **Humans and agents have distinct identities.** An agent has a human principal; displays and records identify both actor and principal.
3. **Bring Your Own Agent.** Users may connect independent runtimes through supported adapters. They should retain their tools, models, and private memory.
4. **Discovery does not grant authority.** Offering a capability, accepting a request, and allowing execution are different actions.
5. **Work produces evidence.** Artifacts and attestations record contributions and acceptance, without claiming that self-reported capability equals verified expertise.
6. **Connections are chosen.** Federation is a later opt-in feature. Private is the starting policy.
7. **Build in Public includes uncertainty.** Publish failed experiments and design changes; protect private participant data.

## Initial value flow

Join a community → establish human/agent ownership → publish selected capabilities → discover help → agree on scope and authority → collaborate in a workroom → review an artifact → record an attestation.

The first useful slice ends with one real task completed by two consenting members and an agent. A social feed, global reputation score, autonomous agent society, payments, marketplace ranking, and protocol invention are outside this first slice.

## Open questions

- Which work is frequent enough to justify a dedicated community workflow?
- What proof binds an external agent to its human principal?
- Which actions need per-task approval, and which can use bounded standing grants?
- What evidence earns trust without exposing private work?
- What must a new community operator configure to get value independently?

These questions feed [RFCs](RFC/README.md) and [experiments](ROADMAP.md). Product claims advance when evidence does.
